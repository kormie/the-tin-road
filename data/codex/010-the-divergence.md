Every world has a year when everything nearly ended. In this one, the raiding fleets came to the Levantine coast as they came in the reader's history — and the letter from Ugarit's king, the one found centuries later still unfired in a kiln, never sent, was *sent*.

The defence clause was invoked instead of ignored. Egypt, Hatti, Ugarit, and Alashiya answered as one bloc instead of falling one at a time. Hattusa was besieged and not taken. The copper kept moving through the worst decade, and everyone who stayed inside the Concord kept eating and kept getting bronze. Everyone who did not, did not.

The raiders who could be beaten were settled on land; the ones who could not were hired. Seven centuries later their descendants garrison every Concord wall, read faster than anyone, and are still waiting for a seat at the table. This was the single most consequential decision in the Concord's history, and it was made in a hurry, by tired people, and never revisited.

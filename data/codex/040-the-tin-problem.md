Bronze is roughly nine parts copper and one part tin, and the two ingredients could not be less alike in their politics.

Copper is close and abundant: Alashiya alone can supply the eastern sea, and never lets anyone forget it. Tin comes from the ends of the earth — Badakhshan beyond the Elamite roads, or the drowned-cold islands past the western intermediaries who lie about their sources. A civilisation running on bronze therefore runs on a supply line four thousand kilometres long for one ingredient, and a short jealous monopoly for the other.

This is not an invention. It is the actual arrangement of the actual Bronze Age, and it is the best thing this world has. Whoever holds tin holds everything. Whoever holds copper holds a great deal, and knows it, and prices accordingly.

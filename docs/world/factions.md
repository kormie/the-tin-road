# Factions

Four power blocs, and the interesting thing is that they do not line up. A merchant house can be Egyptian by charter, Ugaritic by staff, financed by an Assyrian, and in litigation against its own temple. Nobody in this setting has exactly one loyalty.

Every faction entry ends with **what they commission**, because in `The Tin Road` a faction is functionally a source of contracts. If a faction cannot generate a run, it does not need to exist yet.

---

## The Six Seats

The Concord Assembly. Meets every seventh year at a rotating city. Decisions require five of six, which means any two seats acting together can block anything, which means almost nothing passes and the system's stability is largely a function of its paralysis.

### Kemet (Egypt)
Founding signatory. The oldest institution in the world and comfortable telling you so. Controls the Nile grain surplus, which is the Concord's actual insurance policy against a bad decade anywhere else.

Conservative by reflex, and the seat most invested in the treaty remaining a sacred object rather than a readable text. Egyptian temple scribes have the most to lose from road script and have been the loudest about it for two centuries.

**Commissions:** grain convoy escort, retrieval of documents from rival archives, quiet suppression of inconvenient printed material.

### Hatti
Founding signatory. Holds the Anatolian plateau, the overland routes, and the Anatolian tin workings, which are poor but domestic. `[real]` Has the region's oldest ironworking tradition and has spent six centuries not taking it seriously.

The seat most likely to break something. Hatti's tin is running out, its iron is worthless by current values, and it is watching Assur get rich on the Eastern Road.

**Commissions:** prospecting expeditions, route survey into the interior, escort through territory where Hatti's writ has gone thin.

### Ugarit
Admitted during the Crisis. The wealthiest city in the world and structurally the weakest seat, because its power is entirely commercial and can be relocated.

Home of road script, the great houses, and the press. Ugarit's rulers have spent forty years discovering that they do not control the press and cannot.

**Commissions:** everything. Ugarit is the default contract source and the game's likely home city.

### Alashiya (Cyprus)
Admitted during the Crisis. Copper. That is the entire basis of the seat and everyone knows it.

Jealous, insular, and acutely aware that its monopoly is one good ore strike away from ending. Hosts a large Settled population and manages it worse than anyone.

**Commissions:** monopoly enforcement, sabotage of competing copper sources, suppression of survey data.

### Ahhiyawa (the Mycenaean states)
Admitted at the Widening. Not really one polity — a loose federation of palace-states that agree on very little except that the seat should not be given up.

The most maritime, the most fractious, and the most willing to deal outside the Concord entirely. Half the Western Tin Road passes through Ahhiyawan hands and none of it is properly declared.

**Commissions:** anything requiring plausible deniability, western route contact, smuggling that the Assembly would prefer not to hear about.

### Assur
Admitted by negotiation at ~300 AK. Holds the Eastern Tin Road and has never once let anyone forget it.

The newest seat, the most disciplined administration, and the one openly interested in what a post-Concord order might look like with Assur at the centre of it.

**Commissions:** Eastern Road logistics, debt enforcement at range, mapping of alternative routes to Badakhshan.

## The Great Houses

`[real]` Named for the merchant archives actually excavated at Ugarit. Their owners were real people whose letters and ledgers survive. In this timeline the houses were never buried and the descendants still hold the books.

Chartered since ~620 AK, meaning they have legal personality independent of any family. They can hold property, sue, and outlive everyone in them. The player's house is a seventh, minor and recently chartered.

### House Urtenu
The largest. Eastern Road specialists, deeply entangled with Assur, and the closest thing the Concord has to a bank. If you are in debt to anyone, it is probably Urtenu.

Runs the most complete private archive outside a temple, and rents access to it. This makes them the setting's information broker and a natural rival for a player house built on documentation.

### House Rapanu
Old, prestigious, and in slow decline. Traditional temple-script bookkeeping, excellent connections, poor liquidity. Has been quietly selling routes to newer houses for two generations while maintaining that it is doing nothing of the sort.

The house most likely to offer a player something valuable for reasons it will not explain.

### House Yabninu
Aggressive, new money by the standards of a six-hundred-year-old institution, and the first great house to fund a press outright. Bets on road script, cheap reproduction, and volume.

The natural ally for an ambitious player house and an unsentimental one. Yabninu will back you until backing you stops paying.

## The Temples

Not one faction. A loose interest group that agrees on exactly one thing: the authoritative copy of any document should live in a temple.

Their power was never theological. `[real]` Land title, succession, contract, and treaty obligation were all settled by producing the correct tablet, and temples held the tablets. A press does not disprove anything. It just makes the temple's copy one copy among many, and the temples have no answer to that beyond insisting louder.

Internally split three ways:

**The Restorationists** want printing of sacred and legal text banned outright. Losing.

**The Accommodationists** want temples to operate their own presses and reclaim authority through volume. Winning slowly, and hated by the Restorationists more than the merchants are.

**The Archivists** have concluded that authority now belongs to whoever can prove provenance rather than whoever holds the original, and are quietly building the intellectual apparatus for authentication. They are the most dangerous and the least noticed.

**Commissions:** verification of disputed documents, recovery of originals, discreet investigation of a press's output.

## The Settled

Descendants of the peoples absorbed during the Crisis, six hundred years ago. Farmers, sailors, and the backbone of every Concord garrison. Chartered for local self-administration since ~480 AK. Still without an Assembly seat.

They are also the most rapidly literate population in the Concord, because road script is cheap and the press does not check your ancestry. In 735 AK someone printed the Treaty of Kadesh where anyone could read it, and the Settled read it, and discovered that the document does not contain most of the obligations that have been imposed on them.

They have been petitioning formally since 740 AK. The Assembly has been deferring since 740 AK.

**Design note:** resist making them straightforwardly sympathetic or straightforwardly threatening. They are a large, internally divided population with legitimate grievances, effective organisation, and no agreement whatsoever about what they actually want. Some want the seat. Some want the charter enforced as written. Some want out.

**Commissions:** courier work the Assembly would intercept, printing runs nobody will admit to funding, evidence-gathering on charter violations.

## Cross-cutting: the Press Guild

Not a faction so much as an infrastructure everyone is fighting over. Block-cutters, ink makers, and the small number of people who understand how to set a page.

Guild membership crosses every other line here, which makes the guild hall the one room in the setting where a temple archivist, an Assyrian factor, and a Settled organiser might all be standing at the same time, pretending not to notice each other.

Good place to start a session.

# Chronology

Years After Kadesh. 0 AK = 1259 BCE. `[real]` marks attested history.

The shape to hold in your head: a crisis survived, four centuries of boring prosperity that nobody alive remembers as anything but normal, then eighty years of accelerating strain that the game is set inside.

---

## The Founding — 0 to 80 AK

**0 AK / 1259 BCE — The Treaty** `[real]`
Ramesses II and Hattusili III sign. Mutual defence, extradition, non-aggression, sworn before both pantheons. Silver tablets exchanged.

**~30 AK — The Marriage** `[real]`
A Hittite princess marries Ramesses, formalising the peace as kinship. In our history this was the high-water mark and the relationship coasted downhill from there.

**~55 AK — The Grain Shipments** `[real]`
Egypt ships grain to a Hatti suffering serious harvest failure. Attested in correspondence. Here it becomes precedent: the treaty is understood to cover famine as well as war, which is a much larger commitment than the text actually makes.

## The Crisis — 80 to 160 AK

**~80 AK — The Clause Invoked**
The first coordinated raiding season hits the Levantine coast. Ugarit's king sends the letter that in our history was found unfired in a kiln, never dispatched. Here it arrives. The defence clause is invoked for the first time in its history.

**~85 AK — The Concord Fleet**
Egypt, Hatti, Ugarit, and Alashiya operate as a single naval command for four seasons. Militarily unimpressive, logistically unprecedented. It is the logistics that matter: the copper keeps moving.

**~90 AK — Hattusa Holds**
The Hittite capital is besieged and not taken. In our timeline it burned. This single fact is why there is still a Hittite state to sit at the Assembly six hundred years later.

**~95 to 140 AK — The Settlement**
Rather than pursue extermination, the Concord grants land to the raiders it has beaten and hires the ones it has not. Coastal Levant, southern Cyprus, and the Nile delta receive substantial settled populations under vassal terms. `[real]` Egypt did a version of this. The Concord's version is deliberate, systematic, and about ten times larger.

**~160 AK — The Widening**
The Mycenaean palace-states, having watched the alternative, petition to join. The Concord becomes an alliance of six rather than a treaty between two. The Assembly is founded, meeting every seventh year at a rotating city.

## The Long Quiet — 160 to 560 AK

Four centuries in which very little happens that anyone bothered to write down, which is itself the most remarkable thing about this timeline.

**~230 AK — The Assembly Codified**
Rules of procedure fixed. The recitation of the Thousand and the Thousand becomes mandatory. Assembly law starts accumulating precedent, and the temples become its archive by default.

**~300 AK — Assur Admitted**
The sixth seat, and the only one added by negotiation rather than crisis. Assur brings the Eastern Tin Road with it and has been leveraging that fact ever since.

**~360 AK — The Bronze Settles**
Iron working is well understood and deliberately confined to agriculture and the frontier. Not by decree, by economics: tin is cheap enough that nobody serious wants an iron sword.

**~400 AK — Road Script Spreads**
The Ugaritic alphabet, standardised by merchants to work across six languages, becomes the default for anything commercial. Temple scribes regard it as vulgar and unreliable, which it is, and continue to control everything that matters legally.

**~480 AK — The Settled Charters**
After two generations of pressure, the Settled populations receive local self-administration in exchange for permanent garrison obligation. No Assembly seat. The compromise holds for two hundred years and buys the resentment time to compound.

## The Pressure — 560 to 710 AK

**~560 AK — Volume Outruns the Scribes**
Four centuries of uninterrupted trade produce documentation loads the temple scribal system was never designed for. Merchant houses begin keeping serious private archives in road script because the official channel is too slow to be useful.

**~620 AK — The Great Houses**
Urtenu, Rapanu, and Yabninu formalise as chartered houses with legal personality separate from their families. They can now hold property, sue, and outlive their founders. This is arguably a bigger innovation than the press and nobody noticed at the time.

**~680 AK — Block Contracts**
Ugarit's seal-cutters begin producing carved relief blocks for standard contract forms. Purely commercial, entirely boring, wildly profitable. Nobody involved thinks of it as a technology.

## The Press Era — 710 AK to present

**710 AK — The Concord Press**
The block technique reaches literary and religious text. Within fifteen years there are presses in five cities and no agreement anywhere about which copy of anything is authoritative.

**~725 AK — The First Forgery Trial**
A land title produced in three identical copies by three claimants. The Assembly has no procedure for this. The case is still cited and still unresolved.

**~735 AK — The Treaty Printed**
Someone prints the Treaty of Kadesh in road script, cheaply, in quantity. For the first time in seven centuries, ordinary literate people read what the founding document actually says. It says considerably less than the Concord has been claiming.

**~740 AK — The Settled Petitions**
Now the most rapidly literate population in the Concord, the Settled formally petition for a seventh seat, citing the printed treaty text. The Assembly defers. It has been deferring for a decade.

**~750 AK — Present Day**
Iron is finally cheap enough to matter. The presses are winning readers away from the temples faster than anyone expected. A generation raised entirely inside stability wants things their grandparents never needed to ask for.

The Concord has held for seven and a half centuries and is currently held together by the fact that nobody has yet worked out what would replace it.

# Vertical Slice

**The question this build answers:** does documenting a road and then watching a caravan run it unattended feel good?

If yes, the game works and everything else is execution. If that moment lands flat, no amount of setting, class design, or faction politics rescues it. Build the smallest thing that can answer the question honestly, then answer it.

---

## In scope

**Two seasons, minimum.** One to document a route, one to watch it automate. A single-season slice cannot test the core hypothesis and is therefore worthless for this purpose.

- One route: Ugarit to Alashiya and back. Three legs.
- Six nodes total. Two settlements, two hazards, one rival encounter, one ruin.
- The Ledger, complete. All four entry types, two media types (clay and papyrus).
- Automation: three surveys → documented route → assign caravan → passive return in season two.
- The Scribe, partial: Seals, Standing Contracts, and the Courier. Nothing else.
- Death and succession. Season one must be able to kill you.
- One commissioning faction: House Yabninu.

## Explicitly out of scope

Cutting these is not deferral, it is the point of a slice.

- The Keeper and Endurance. Season one is solo.
- Archive corruption. Test the reward loop before testing the system that poisons it.
- Factors, Obligations, Conversion.
- Presses, Copy, Publish, Forge.
- The Concord axis and any world state at all.
- Five of six seats. Faction politics is content, not hypothesis.
- Art beyond legibility. Placeholder everything.

## Build order

1. Season clock, Daylight spend, and node traversal. Nothing else.
2. The Ledger. Writing must cost real Daylight and produce visible archive entries.
3. Death, and the successor inheriting an archive.
4. The Courier, which is what makes death survivable rather than punitive.
5. Automation. Assign a caravan, watch it return.

Step 5 is the whole build. Steps 1 through 4 exist to make step 5 mean something.

## What to measure

Instrument the playtest for these specifically. Impressions after the fact are not usable data here.

| Signal | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Daylight spent writing vs travelling | Whether the central tension is live or one option dominates |
| Whether they survey a full route unprompted | Whether the automation goal is legible without being told |
| Reaction at first automated return | The hypothesis, answered. Watch faces, not surveys. |
| Whether they use the Courier | Whether the cost is calibrated or prohibitive |
| Whether season two feels different from season one | Whether inheritance reads as progress or as a reset |

## Failure conditions

Be willing to see these and act on them.

**Players optimise writing away.** If skipping the Ledger and running fast is dominant, the season clock is too tight or the archive rewards too little. Fixable with tuning, but if it survives three tuning passes the loop is wrong.

**Automation feels like the game playing itself.** The pleasure should be that you built it. If the automated return reads as an idle-game trickle instead of a payoff, the surveying was too cheap or the route was too short to feel earned.

**Inheritance reads as a reset.** If season two feels like starting over rather than standing on something, the archive is not carrying enough forward and the whole generational premise is in trouble.

## After the slice

Only if the hypothesis holds.

1. The Keeper and Endurance, which is the second-largest untested assumption in the design.
2. Archive corruption, on probation, with a clean cut path.
3. The remaining Scribe kit.
4. Factions as commission variety.
5. The Concord axis last. It is a campaign-length system and cannot be validated short.
